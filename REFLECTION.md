## CrimsonCode Hackathon Reflection

Earlier this year I attended the CrimsonCode Hackathon. Around this time I was really into older Mac systems and what their vulnerabilities / flaws were that made them suseptible. I spent a good time researching about older macs in the morning and used a lot of time looking at some of the CVE's that were existent of that error of Mac Os 9. Under the AppleScripts folder can see a script that exists which finds the Users & Groups Data File ans then deletes it. The purpose being once deleted the Mac would assume whatever user was "Owner" on next login giving someone full admin with no password.

I then after creating a MacOS 9 virtual machine had to seek out the popular IDE of the time which was CodeWarriors and then convert the internet acrchive files for it to actually run which required my current Mac mounting and reconverting it to use the correct storage format and make it so it could be mounted. But after all of that I was able to install and play around with it, there was a sample called SillyBalls which kind of show how some of the library and system calls work at the time. I found out a lot of modern graphics as I know didn't work on this really old mac. I exported the SillyBalls project and then began making a modern game with the premise and story being a user is playing a ConnectFour game on their older Mac but things go wrong and start being "Hacked". It was meant to connect into some of the viruses of the time on Mac systems and was made with fun.

I think technically I learned a lot about older Macs and Virtualization especially on older architecture that is different than your own systems and the difficulties of finding older software that you want.

I participated by myself this year and got to kind of just take a moment to research and learn about cyber security and more about Macs which is fitting since it is the 50th anniversity of Apple. I had a few senior friends present at the event who were volunteering, I kind of assisted by showing some younger comp-sci students how to install and use GIT along with various things. As well my friend Martin who works at SEL and was a volunteer showed me his embedded system car and how he used path finding algorithms with it and we may have used it to drive around the Spark for a little break.

I think my assesement is fair, I think I could have maybe produced a greater delieverable but to me the Hackathon is also part of the WSU Computer Science community coming together as a whole. I enjoyed being able to talk to my peers some of the professors who were there like Andrew O’Fallon and I think that time at the event was just as valuable. Being a senior with a postgrad job I think the event I wouldn't say outgrown but is more beneficial for younger students. During my time at WSU being a 2022 admitted student it was interesting having a time before AI and where AI was not used at Hackathons. If I could redo I would have poured water on my good friend Simon who said he needed to sleep instead of attend; would have been fun to try to offer a more competitive product. But to me it was a good time to learn with no stress attached and got to read old papers.
